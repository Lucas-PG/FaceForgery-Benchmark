# Does the same forgery stay detectable after video redistribution?

This branch implements a recording-level study of visual face-forgery detection for forensic triage. A detector sees one received video at a time. The experimenter knows which copies came from the same recording. A forgery counts as reliably detected when every registered copy triggers review. A genuine recording incurs a false alert when any copy triggers review. The primary false-alert budget is 5% of genuine recording lineages. The finite panel of routes defines the scope of each result.

The SIBGRAPI and TCC experiments studied detector rankings and marginal robustness, chiefly with image-level MFFI and simulated degradation. Different forgeries can remain detectable on different routes even when the average scores agree. This campaign asks whether that distinction changes the operating decision, how much a frozen score can recover through route-specific thresholds, and whether learning from correct copy correspondence improves repeatable detection.

**The hypothesis** is that matched-copy training can improve all-copy sensitivity on independent recorded populations and a new transmission route while maintaining native recall and controlling genuine any-copy errors. The threshold ceiling supplies a falsifiable test. If any threshold policy respects the any-copy genuine budget, its false alarms on each individual route must also respect that budget. Each permissible cutoff is therefore bounded below by the corresponding genuine score quantile. The fraction of forgeries above all of those lower limits bounds what route-specific monotone recalibration of that frozen detector could achieve. Independent genuine diagnostic lineages and sealed forgery lineages provide a conservative confidence bound.

**The experiment.** Source training uses FF++ footage with genuine, locally encoded, and resized/encoded views. Source calibration also has a measured delivery route. Independent DFDC and KoDF recordings receive two measured deliveries, including one withheld from source development and calibration. The registered KoDF FSGAN stratum tests a named held-out family. Frozen GenD, LipForensics and archival DINO/CLIP provide external references. Within each matched backbone the new intervention competes with ordinary augmented training, worst-view training, and deliberately broken correspondence. DINO additionally supplies the low-FPR ranking control and two constraint ablations. Production acquisition includes human-reviewed lineage and a tracked-face diagnostic. Each case remains identifiable throughout the publication outputs.

**Interpreting the result.** A learning claim requires a material sensitivity gain, native noninferiority, genuine false-alert control, comparison against actual recalibrators and established training controls, and evidence for correspondence. Exceeding the recalibration ceiling is a separate, stronger finding. Report the primary seed, replication, seed variation and frame/crop failures explicitly. A change in detector selection with independent consequences can be valuable even if paired training fails. Underpowered intervals, acquisition failures, or effects confined to one cohort limit the claim. Engineering tests produce no evidence for the scientific hypothesis.
